Twenty-odd years I ran delivery inside big companies, the last ten of them on AI work, back when "AI transformation" still meant building the damn thing and not putting it on a slide. Long enough to figure out that most process is theater, and the actual work usually gets done by a handful of people who give a damn.

Now it is just me.

A one-person AI product studio, building whatever has my attention that week, with a pile of AI agents doing the typing while I point them at things.

No ceremony. No roadmap committee. I build it, run it, see if it holds, and fix what breaks. The repos here are ugly and they work. I only care about the second part.

Somewhere along the way I also wrote a book, Please Take This Banana As Payment: Everything You Should've Been Taught About Money But Weren't (Until Now) (https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FBMLN3W4), a plain-English guide to money, economics, inflation, debt, banking, and why the world works the way it does.

When I am not building, I am overthinking things at Null Velocity (https://www.nullvelocity.com), writing essays about the odd little details of everyday life: filter coffee, technology, and whatever else refuses to leave my head.

No README on a repo means it did not earn one.
